# Narrative (叙事)

Tell a story, not a list. A short explainer is a chain of questions: **hook → question → why it's hard → the surprising
clue → how it works → what it unlocked → the human payoff → the lift**.

- **Chain the scenes**: end each scene on a question or an incomplete thought that the next scene answers
  (「要是有一道光控闸门……」 → 「答案，藏在一个池塘里」). If a scene can be swapped with another, it isn't chained.
- **One through-line**: an object or character that returns (the mouse with the fibre, the ghost particle, the light).
  Call back to it at least once mid-film and once at the end.
- **People, not just facts**: who asked the question, what they were curious about — curiosity is the emotional engine.
- **Length**: vertical shorts ≤ 60 s unless asked otherwise. Cut whole beats rather than speeding everything up.
  When over length, drop side-quests (extra history, extra applications) before the core mechanism.
- **Reading load**: Chinese on screen ≈ 5–7 characters per second; one caption at a time; hold ≥ 1.2 s.
- **Truth**: verify every number and claim before it goes on screen; label demonstrations ("实验示意") and paraphrases ("大意").
- **Topic first**: when the user asks for a video about a broad subject, offer a ranked shortlist of angles before building.
