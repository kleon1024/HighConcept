## The idea
**命题：「人类为什么需要一个鬼节？」** The film opens on a fact most viewers don't know: the jack-o'-lantern was a
turnip before it was a pumpkin. It follows Halloween from Ireland to America, from fun to money. Then it turns: the
pumpkin's home is Mexico, where the dead are welcomed home, and the East has its own nights of the dead (中元 / 盂兰盆,
お盆). All of these festivals light a lamp to guide the dead, so **one flame** runs through the film. It lights a
turnip, a pumpkin, an ofrenda candle, a river lantern and a paper lantern; at the end all five glow in a row.
Answer: 「怕鬼，其实是想念。」

Style: vintage Halloween postcards (c. 1908–10, orange on black), paper-cut night scenes, and real prints and photos
for every culture: Maclise, Hokusai, Posada, a 1915 photo of 中元 water lanterns, an 1862 print of lantern boats, and
Kyoto's 送り火. Folklore is flagged on screen (传说, 民间说法) and forecasts as 预计.

Music: one "ghost-lantern" motif written in the spirit of Saint-Saëns's *Danse macabre* (1874, public domain; nothing
quoted): a tritone call (A–E♭), a limping chromatic walk (G–F–E–E♭–D) and an unresolved step home. It changes
costume with each culture:
- an Irish jig
- an organ with the Dies irae
- a parlour harpsichord
- a theremin gag
- cartoon bassoon
- son jarocho in major
- 箫 and 木鱼
- a bon-odori

On 「怕鬼，其实是想念」 the tritone finally resolves into G major.
