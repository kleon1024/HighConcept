# NIHIL · 虚无 — 60 秒，黑底哲学

16:9 · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · **60 s** (120 BPM) · Remotion composition `Nihil` (`src/nihil/`)
Timing and cues: `src/nihil/nihil.json` (shared with the score). Score and sound design: `music/nihil.py` (cue sheet in
`out/nihil_cues.md`). Image credits: `media/MEDIA_CREDITS.md` (`me_nietzsche`, `nh_monk`).

## The idea
One word, 「意义」, set huge on black. In the first second it cracks and **blows away as dust**. The film is the
journey from that dust back to the word: what nihilism is, where the word came from, Nietzsche's diagnosis (God is
dead, the highest values devalue themselves), the two nihilisms (passive vs active), the abyss, the eternal return,
amor fati. At the end the same dust **gathers back into 「意义」, now gold**. The line that lands: 虚无，不是深渊——是画布。

Style: black ground, white serif type, hairlines, one point of light. No figures are drawn. The only images are a real
photograph of Nietzsche and Friedrich's *Monk by the Sea*. Colour arrives only once, as gold, at amor fati, and it stays
through the ending.

Leitmotif: the falling figure **G–E♭–C–B** (the word blowing away, the fall after "God is dead"). It is answered by
the rising **C–E–G–C** (rebuild, become who you are, the ending in C major).

## Facts and sources
| On screen | Source / note |
|---|---|
| *nihil* = "nothing" | Latin |
| 1799 Jacobi brings 「虚无主义」 into philosophical debate | F. H. Jacobi, open letter to Fichte (*Jacobi an Fichte*, 1799), charging idealism with *Nihilismus*. Earlier isolated uses exist (Obereit 1787, Jenisch 1796), so the film does not claim "first" |
| 1862 虚无主义者：不向任何权威低头 | Turgenev, *Fathers and Sons* (1862), ch. 5: a nihilist "does not bow to any authority, does not accept any principle on faith" |
| 1880 「没有上帝，就什么都可以」（大意） | Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov* (1879–80). The idea is voiced by Ivan and repeated by others. The famous one-line wording is a paraphrase, hence 大意 |
| 「上帝死了。」 *Gott ist tot*; "Are we not plunging continually? Backward, sideward, forward, in all directions? Is there still any up or down?" | Nietzsche, *The Gay Science* §125 (1882) |
| The highest values devalue themselves | Nietzsche's notebooks 1887 (*Will to Power* §2) |
| Passive vs active nihilism | notebooks 1887 (*Will to Power* §22–23): nihilism as decline of the spirit's power (passive) vs as a sign of its increased power (active) |
| Philosophizing with a hammer: sounding out idols to hear that they are hollow | *Twilight of the Idols, or How to Philosophize with a Hammer* (written 1888, published 1889), Preface |
| 「当你凝视深渊，深渊也在凝视你。」 | *Beyond Good and Evil* §146 (1886) |
| The demon, "this life … innumerable times more", "this spider and this moonlight between the trees", gnashing teeth vs "never have I heard anything more divine" | *The Gay Science* §341, "The Greatest Weight" (1882) |
| *amor fati*: not merely bear what is necessary, but love it | *The Gay Science* §276 (1882); *Ecce Homo*, "Why I Am So Clever" §10 (1888) |
| 「你必须心中有混沌，才能生出一颗跳舞的星。」 | *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*, Prologue §5 (1883) |
| 「成为你自己。」 | *The Gay Science* §270 (1882); subtitle of *Ecce Homo: How One Becomes What One Is* (1888) |
| *The Monk by the Sea* | Caspar David Friedrich, *Der Mönch am Meer* (1808–1810), Alte Nationalgalerie, Berlin |
| 「意义……是你从虚无里，亲手造出来的」「虚无是画布」 | the film's own reading of Nietzsche's revaluation of values, not a quotation |
