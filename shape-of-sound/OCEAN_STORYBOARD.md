## The idea
In 1992, 28,800 bath toys fell into the North Pacific. They washed up where the currents carried them, and
oceanographers used them to test their models. The film follows them as a through-line. Its subject is the
counter-intuitive fact that **the biggest rivers on Earth are in the sea**. It shows how big they are, why they flow
and where they carry things. It then follows the slower river in the deep, which takes about a thousand years to go
round. It ends on a Chinese line everyone knows: Li Bai's river that "runs to the sea and never returns".

The current travels the world, so the film does too. Every act takes the art style of the place or era it visits:
- Hokusai's wave
- NASA's *Perpetual Ocean*
- Franklin's engraved chart
- Winslow Homer
- a Swiss poster
- the Bauhaus
- Hokusai's Prussian blue again for the duck map
- Haeckel's plates for the deep
- a Victorian engraving
- an ECG trace
- Ma Yuan's *Water Album* for the ending

Three things run through all of them:
- **The river**: a luminous stream with comets of light. It is painted onto Franklin's chart, becomes the bars of the
  Swiss poster, the Kuroshio, the duck route, the conveyor, the heartbeat, and the loop drawn over Ma Yuan's water.
- **The yellow duck**: a vector toy, not a photograph.
- **The theme**: one 12/8 sea tune that changes instruments in every act.
