# 失真 DISTORTION — 30 s art short (1080×1920, 30 fps)

Style: B2 · acid graphics (liquid chrome, near-monochrome + one acid lime `#C8FF1E`, hairline grids, micro-type, grain).
Style research and frame: `styleframes/b2_acid/` (`research.md`, `refs/board.jpg`, `frame.png`, `notes.md`).

## 立意 (the one idea)

**「失真，是最真的声音。」** — Rock's defining sound was not designed; it leaked out of broken speakers. The damage became
the voice. (Pun: 失真 literally means "losing fidelity / losing the truth".)

## Theme and emotional arc

Shock (a blade opens a chrome speaker) → curiosity (why would anyone do that?) → understanding (a signal pushed past its
rails is clipped, and the clipping *is* the sound) → a chain of accidents turning into intention (1951 damaged amp →
1958 pencil holes → 1962 the damage sold in a box → 1964 the razor) → release (a whole genre: a wall of speakers, double
kick) → resolution and pride (the wound becomes the name: 「失真」), then a breath.

## Hook (0–1 s)

Frame 1: extreme close-up of a mirror-chrome speaker cone; a razor blade is already crossing it. 0.0–0.4 s the cut opens
and acid-lime light spills out of the wound (sound from frame 1: amp hum + the rip). **0.75 s: the first distorted power
chord slams** with kick + crash and the camera is knocked back. Impact + counter-intuition: someone is destroying the
thing that makes the sound — and it sounds *better*.

## How the acid style serves the theme

- **Chrome** is literally the metal of amps, speaker baskets and blades, and the visual language of metal music.
- **Liquid chrome** = a signal melting: surfaces ripple harder on every hit, wounds heal in "rewind".
- **The clip rails** (two lime hairlines) are the mechanism itself: flat headline type and waveforms are cut flat by them.
- **The one acid colour** is the sound leaking out of the damage — it only ever comes *out of a wound* (cut, cracks, holes).
- **Micro-type and grids** turn the history into an engineering log: signal chain, gain, dates.

## Music

Metal at **160 BPM** (bar = 1.5 s), E minor/phrygian power chords. The riff downbeat grid starts at **t = 0.75 s**
(bar *n* starts at 0.75 + 1.5·(n−1)). The amp-sim **gain follows the history**: crunch (1951/58) → fuzz (1962) →
full saturation (1964) → high-gain metal with double kick (genre) → one huge final chord.
FreePats DI guitar (CC0) re-amped by our own numpy amp (pre-EQ, asymmetric clipping, cab filtering), double-tracked
L/R; FreePats picked bass (CC0); FreePats MuldjordKit acoustic metal kit (CC BY 4.0).

## Transition chain (every cut carries something)

1. **T1 2.25 s** wound → wave: zoom-through the cut; the lime line inside the wound *is* the sine wave of the next shot.
2. **T2 5.25 s** wave → speaker: the clipped wave (chrome tube) curls into a ring that becomes the speaker rim (liquid morph);
   the wound heals in reverse = rewind to 1951.
3. **T3 8.25 s** 1951 → 1958: same speaker, glitch/slice cut on the beat; the cracks stay, the pencil enters.
4. **T4 11.25 s** 1958 → 1962: zoom into the last pencil hole (a circle) → the hole is the stompbox knob (circle match).
5. **T5 14.25 s** 1962 → 1964: the maxed knob matches the speaker's dust cap (ring match); the razor returns.
6. **T6 17.25 s** 1964 → genre: zoom-through the wound → warp-grid dive through concentric ribs → wall of speakers.
7. **T7 24.75 s** genre → title: the lime clip rails slam the whole frame flat on the final chord; the line opens into 「失真」.

## Ending 升华

Bookend: the hook's wound returns as the name of the sound. The frame widens from one cut speaker to a wall of them,
then collapses to the single word 「失真」 with the resonant line **「失真，是最真的声音」** (*the truest sound is the broken
one*) on the biggest chord, then ~1.5 s of breath as the chrome settles.

## Facts used on screen (verified, see sources)

- **1951** "Rocket 88" (Jackie Brenston & his Delta Cats): guitarist Willie Kizart's amp was damaged on the way to Sam
  Phillips' studio, the speaker cone burst and was stuffed with paper; one of the first recorded distorted guitars.
  [Wikipedia: Rocket 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_88)
- **1958** "Rumble": Link Wray poked holes in his amp's speaker with a pencil.
  [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/link-wray-rumble-legacy), [Wikipedia: Rumble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumble_(instrumental))
- **1962** the Maestro FZ-1 Fuzz-Tone, the first widely marketed fuzz effect. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maestro_FZ-1_Fuzz-Tone)
- **1964** "You Really Got Me": Dave Davies cut the speaker cone of his small Elpico amp with a razor blade.
  [Wikipedia: Dave Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Davies)
On screen these appear as big dates plus three micro-type lines each (exact text, `src/main.js`):
- 1951 — ROCKET 88 · JACKIE BRENSTON & HIS DELTA CATS / GUITAR AMP DAMAGED ON THE ROAD → CONE BURST / STUFFED WITH PAPER · RECORDED ANYWAY
- 1958 — RUMBLE · LINK WRAY / HOLES PUNCHED IN THE SPEAKER WITH A PENCIL / DAMAGE ON PURPOSE
- 1962 — MAESTRO FZ-1 FUZZ-TONE / THE SOUND OF DAMAGE, SOLD IN A BOX / KNOB → n / 10
- 1964 — YOU REALLY GOT ME · THE KINKS / DAVE DAVIES SLASHES HIS AMP’S CONE WITH A RAZOR / THE RIFF THAT TAUGHT ROCK TO GROWL (paraphrase)
- CLIP — SIGNAL > RAIL = CLIPPED / FLAT TOPS → ODD HARMONICS / THE SOUND OF OVERLOAD (symmetric clipping adds odd harmonics)

The fall in 1951 is a visual metaphor for "damaged on the road" (accounts differ: fell from the car roof vs. rain in the
trunk), so the label says only DAMAGED ON THE ROAD. Brand logos are never drawn; the pedal on screen is a generic
chrome box labelled FUZZ.
